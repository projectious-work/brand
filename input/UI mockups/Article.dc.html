Provider independence in an agentic stack

A framework for keeping AI vendor choices reversible without slowing delivery.

Marius Keller·Aug 4, 2026·12 min read

Most teams treat their model provider as a foundation decision, made once and revisited only under duress. That is backwards. The provider is the layer most likely to change — through pricing, capability shifts, or policy — and the layer least worth being locked into.

The switching cost is architectural, not contractual

A contract can be cancelled in a quarter. A codebase that calls one provider's SDK directly from forty places cannot. The real lock-in lives in how many places assume one vendor's request and response shape.

We route every model call through one internal interface, regardless of how many providers sit behind it:

// one interface, swappable backends
interface ModelClient {
  complete(req: CompletionRequest): Promise<CompletionResult>;
}
 
const client = resolveClient(policy.preferredProvider);
ⓘ

The interface, not the model, is the thing worth testing. Provider swaps become a config change, not a migration.

What this costs you

Provider independence is not free. It means giving up the newest provider-specific feature on day one, and it means maintaining an abstraction layer someone has to own. In our experience, that cost is worth paying for anything that touches production data or customer-facing decisions — and not worth paying for a weekend prototype.

“The provider is the layer most likely to change, and the layer least worth being locked into.”

Where we draw the line

Self-host where the workload is stable and the data is sensitive. Use a hosted frontier model where the task benefits from the newest capability and the data is not. Reassess quarterly, not annually — the tradeoff moves faster than most procurement cycles.
